# Evolve Y — First Pilot Brief

Concept edition • 14 October 2026 • Proposed, not launched

## Purpose

Test whether an AI-assisted, human-reviewed operations desk can reduce the administrative burden of property maintenance while protecting resident privacy, improving completion and preserving fair payment for providers.

## Initial market

Dallas–Fort Worth. Recruit 10–20 small property operators or maintenance organizations with repeated coordination work and a named person able to authorize purchase of the service. Start with one workflow rather than property acquisition, lending, a token economy or a full exchange.

## Service

1. Collect a request and the minimum necessary evidence with permission.
2. Create a shared project record with role-specific access.
3. Organize documents and prepare questions using AI assistance.
4. Compare estimates and draft updates with links to source evidence.
5. Obtain human approval before committing to cost, scope or a provider.
6. Track the work, verify completion and provide a complaint route.
7. Reconcile invoices using established payment arrangements.
8. Export the record and measure the outcome.

The pilot does not autonomously decide tenant eligibility, make loans, provide legal advice, hold client funds or issue investment interests. Those are distinct activities requiring separate arrangements.

## Customer proposition

A single place to coordinate a project, less repetitive administration and clearer responsibility. The $250/month price used on the website is an illustrative planning assumption, not a current offer. Test willingness to pay and define included volume, review, support, setup and cancellation terms before launch.

## Measurement

Establish a baseline before introducing the service. Compare similar completed projects; document differences and avoid treating a small non-random pilot as causal proof.

- Customer: administrative hours including review, full cost, completion time, quality and renewal.
- Worker/provider: effective pay, payment delay, unpaid coordination effort and access to opportunities.
- Resident: communication clarity, completion quality, privacy and complaint handling.
- Business: model usage, reviewer minutes, support, onboarding, acquisition expense, contribution margin and cash runway.
- Rights: export success, permission violations, appeal time, accessibility and exclusion.

Proposed continuation threshold: at least 8 of the first 10 completing organizations renew on stated terms; a meaningful documented improvement without worse quality; no severe unresolved incident; and a credible path to positive contribution margin after human support. These are planning thresholds, not achieved results.

## Budget discipline

The website’s default one-month model uses 100 paying organizations, $250 per organization, $90 direct cost per organization and $12,000 fixed cost. The pilot is smaller. At 20 customers under those assumptions, revenue is $5,000, direct cost $1,800 and operating loss $8,800 per month. Three months therefore require $26,400 to cover modeled operating losses, before initial product, professional and security work.

This is not a complete budget. Obtain actual estimates. Model working capital, taxes, insurance, acquisition, depreciation and financing where relevant. Secure a bounded runway; do not fund distributions with new customer capital or investment proceeds.

## Sharing and ownership

Pay providers for work as an ordinary operating cost. The concept calculator illustrates a 30% allocation of positive distributable surplus to a shared pool, with 65% of that pool for eligible members and 35% for community access. It does not create binding financial rights.

Before launch, determine the lawful structure, exact eligibility, voting rights, reserve policy, records, disclosure and enforcement. Separate service membership from any investment offering. No existing company charter or investment contract is changed by this proposal. Benefits can be zero. Access for people unable to pay needs its own funded provision.

## Technical starting point

Standard web application, segregated customer records, relational data store, encrypted documents, role-based permissions, a queue for background work and a versioned approval/event history. Keep model access replaceable. Enforce action permissions outside the model. Use current commercial providers before building compute capacity.

Open the project schema and export/import format. Test a real data export and provider switch. Establish backups, deletion and retention rules, incident response, prompt-injection controls, least-privilege credentials and human escalation.

## People and authority

Name a product owner, service delivery lead, reviewer, security/incident owner and independent complaints reviewer. Work with appropriately qualified professionals where required. Agree a participant rights charter. Establish a route for member representation and independent review before growth makes exit costly.

## Stop conditions

Stop affected automation after a severe incident. Narrow or stop the business if customers will not pay, support consumes the margin, quality deteriorates or rights cannot be protected. Publish the findings, including adverse outcomes. Expand to a second locality only when delivery is repeatable without founders handling every case.

## Decisions needed before recruitment

- Exact workflow, customer profile and service limits.
- Actual price and written service/refund terms.
- Names and responsibilities of operators and reviewers.
- Baseline and data collection plan with consent.
- Funded budget and runway.
- Rights charter, member powers and any benefit-sharing structure.
- Launch review and date for a 90-day decision.
