A small, testable beginning.

Scale a service that people choose to use. Every stage should produce evidence, reveal costs and preserve the freedom to change direction.

A roadmap made
of gates, not guarantees.

The dates below describe a possible working sequence. None of these milestones is claimed as achieved.

01

Weeks 1–3, proposed

Discovery

Interview 15–25 small property operators and service providers in DFW. Observe a real workflow. Establish baseline time, cost, error and payment delay.

Evidence to continue

Gate: at least 10 qualified organizations agree to a paid, bounded pilot; identify a named buyer and a recurring problem.

02

90 days, proposed

Concierge pilot

Serve 10–20 organizations with one shared project workflow. Use existing AI and payment tools, human review and simple records. Offer export from day one.

Evidence to continue

Gate: repeat use and paid renewal; no material quality decline; documented time or cost improvement after all review effort.

03

After pilot evidence

Repeatable product

Automate the steps already understood. Standardize data, roles, permissions, event logs and onboarding. Test provider switching and recovery.

Evidence to continue

Gate: positive contribution margin including human support; acquisition payback is credible; independent security and rights review completed.

04

After repeatability

Local federation

Train independent operators in a second region. Give them the same interface, operating handbook, quality measures and member protections.

Evidence to continue

Gate: second-region results hold without founder-led delivery; disputes and incident response remain manageable.

05

Only when justified

Broader ecosystem

Add one adjacent application at a time: GOE opportunities, learning, creator royalties or contracted compute. Consider hubs only after demonstrated occupancy demand.

Evidence to continue

Gate: separate customer demand and unit economics for each service. Financing and regulated activity receive separate review.

Practical scaling

Replicate the method.
Do not multiply the complexity.

Build one reusable coordination core and a clear operating handbook. Add infrastructure only when the existing service creates demand for it.

PRODUCT

One core, bounded modules

A versioned project schema, role-based permissions, task state, approvals and document evidence. Service-specific modules extend the core without turning every pilot into custom software.

OPERATIONS

A repeatable local playbook

Standard onboarding, quality checks, escalation, support limits and training. Local teams keep relationships and context; the network maintains common quality expectations.

ECONOMICS

A contribution-margin gate

Separate recurring delivery from setup work. Track human minutes and model cost per completed project. Increase price or narrow scope when review costs overwhelm value.

Proposed production architecture

Use a standard web application with segregated customer data, a relational database, encrypted object storage and queued background tasks. Store approvals and changes in an append-only event history with a documented correction mechanism. AI providers sit behind a replaceable service interface. Payments stay with established processors.

Before federation, publish a record format, export/import tests and a compatibility policy. Regional operators can eventually run conforming services with local data controls. Conformance tests, independent audits and dispute agreements matter as much as the software.

What should be open, and what earns money?

Open the project formats, portability tools and public governance rules. Charge for dependable hosting, integration, support and execution. Licensed domain materials and confidential customer data stay protected. A defensible business can be built on delivery, trust and expertise rather than trapping records.

Fund the learning period explicitly.

With the calculator’s default costs and 20 customers, three months require roughly $26,400 to cover modeled operating losses, before initial product, legal and security work. This is an illustration—not a budget quote. Scope and price the actual pilot before seeking funding. Keep any investment arrangements separate from service membership.

Permission to change course

A good vision can
survive a failed idea.

Stop or narrow the pilot if customers will not pay, human review consumes the margin, outcomes do not improve or rights cannot be protected. Publish what failed and why. A useful local service is a better result than an unworkable global system.

Do not use token sales, promotional valuations, new member payments or hypothetical acquisitions to fill an unexplained operating deficit. If a compute service is not competitive, buy compute elsewhere. If a module is not needed, leave it out.

The long-term ambition is a network that becomes more useful as people participate—while staying answerable to them.
