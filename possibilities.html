The first proposed service

A shared operations desk
for property and repair work.

Small operators juggle messages, documents, estimates and scheduling across disconnected tools. A reliable coordination service is a problem worth paying to solve.

THE CUSTOMER

Small property operators

Start with organizations managing recurring maintenance or property preparation. They pay a transparent monthly service fee. Contractors are paid for their work through ordinary agreements.

THE SERVICE

AI with accountable people

Collect requests, organize evidence, compare quotes, draft updates and reconcile invoices. A named human checks the work and approves commitments.

THE BENEFIT

Less friction, better completion

Measure administrative hours, turnaround, rework, disputes and payment delay. Publish the full cost, including review and support. A polished demo alone proves none of these.

Proposed pilot boundaries: no autonomous lending, tenant selection, legal advice, property custody or investment activity. These require distinct expertise, authority and operating arrangements.

From a request
to work well done.

This fictional example shows the intended experience. It does not submit a request, book a contractor, use an AI model or move money.

Illustrative service journey

A repair at a small apartment building.

A resident needs a reliable repair, a contractor needs clear instructions and prompt payment, and the operator needs confidence that the work was done. Their interests overlap, but they are not identical.

The platform should coordinate those interests without hiding who decides, who pays or who can challenge the result.

Each participant can export the records they are entitled to access. Sensitive resident details are not exposed to every bidder.

Participation without a buy-in

You should not have to
own capital to belong.

Workers can earn for real work. Small businesses can buy useful services. Researchers can evaluate claims. Communities can shape local priorities. People unable to work or invest must still be included in the purpose of the system.

Start with open learning materials and sponsored access. As revenue permits, dedicate a visible budget to accessibility and community-selected services. Do not require investment, hardware ownership, biometric enrollment or a religious or political affiliation.

Sharing rewards only in proportion to spending would favor those who already have the most. A separate access budget and member voice help prevent contribution-based rewards from becoming the whole account of fairness.

Make the useful thing. Then make it widely available.

The economics must work for the people using it, the people delivering it and the business supporting it.
